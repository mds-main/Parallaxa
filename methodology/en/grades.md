# Grades and claim types

Parallaxa splits what you send into atomic claims and classifies each one:

- **Fact**: an empirical statement that evidence can confirm or contradict.
- **Value**: a moral or political judgement. Values are never graded true or false; we show the strongest arguments for each position instead.
- **Prediction**: a statement about the future. Predictions are tracked, not graded today.
- **Attribution**: "X said Y". We check whether X really said Y, in context.
- **Framing**: loaded language or selective statistics.

## The seven grades

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Well supported | At least two independent sources, including one primary or research source, clearly support it. |
| Mostly supported | Supported, with minor inaccuracies. |
| Missing context | Technically accurate, but misleading without important context. |
| Mixed or contested | The evidence genuinely points both ways, or parts are right and parts wrong. |
| Mostly unsupported | The evidence mostly does not support it. |
| Contradicted by evidence | At least two independent sources, including one primary or research source, clearly contradict it. |
| Not checkable (yet) | No reliable evidence exists yet, or it is a value or prediction. |

Every grade comes with a confidence level (high, medium or low) and the reason for it.

## Truth over balance

We apply symmetric **rules**, not symmetric **outcomes**. If the evidence shows one side is wrong more often on an issue, we say so. "What each side gets right" sections can be empty: we never invent balance.
